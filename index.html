Sweetness without the sugar problem. Made by plants.

We are developing a way to produce brazzein, a naturally sweet protein, in hydroponic lettuce. The goal: a plant-based sweetener at a fair price.

Contact us How it works

The problem

The world needs less sugar. Taste still matters.

<10% of daily energy

The World Health Organization strongly recommends that adults and children keep free sugars below 10% of their total energy intake.[2]

  • Cutting sugar without losing sweetness is a challenge for food and beverage makers.
  • Sweet proteins like brazzein occur in nature but are hard to source at scale.
  • Producing them affordably is the bottleneck we want to solve.

Our approach

Plants as protein factories.

Molecular farming uses plants to produce valuable proteins. We are building a process to make brazzein in lettuce (Lactuca sativa).

  1. Design

    Design the brazzein gene and the plant expression system.

  2. Grow

    Express the protein transiently in hydroponic lettuce through agroinfiltration.

  3. Purify

    Purify with a heat-shock step and confirm with ELISA and Western blot.

Why brazzein

A sweet protein, found in nature.

500–2,000×

Sweeter than sucrose, depending on the comparison concentration.[1]

Plant origin

First identified in the fruit of Pentadiplandra brazzeana, a West African plant. A small protein of 54 amino acids.[1]

4 h at 80 °C

Sweetness was retained after this heat treatment in the original study.[1]
